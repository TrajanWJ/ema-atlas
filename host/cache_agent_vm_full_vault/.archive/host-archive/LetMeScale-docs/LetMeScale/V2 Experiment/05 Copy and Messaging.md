# Copy & Messaging — v2

> Direct but human. Assertive coach energy. A confident person, not a machine.

---

## Brand Voice

### Tone: 6-7/10 — Bold and Direct
Not cold and clinical (that was v1). Not hype-y either. The voice of a high-performance coach who's invested in your success but doesn't need your business.

### Voice Character
- **Direct but human** — Short punchy lines, but written by a person with energy, not a system readout
- **Assertive coach** — "We build winners. Are you one?" Challenging, but invested
- **Confident, not arrogant** — State facts boldly. Let the proof do the bragging
- **No hedging** — Don't use "might," "could," "potentially." Say what it is

### Power Dynamic
LetMeScale is still the gatekeeper. The reader applies for access. But the gate isn't cold — it's selective because the system works best for specific operators.

---

## No Word Restrictions

v1 had banned word lists and prescribed vocabulary. v2 doesn't.

**Write naturally.** If "service" is the right word, use it. If "system" fits better, use that. Don't artificially constrain the copy — just don't be generic.

The only rule: **write well and write boldly.**

---

## Sentence Rhythm

Varied. Don't lock into one pattern.

Mix short, medium, and long sentences. Let the copy breathe.

```
Short:    "We build attention that converts."
Medium:   "Four clients. Three generated six figures. One built a billion-dollar brand."
Long:     "When Trell came to us, his revenue was declining $8K month over month —
           six weeks later, he pulled $83K in a single sprint."
```

Headlines should punch. Body copy should flow. Let each piece of text do what it needs to do.

## Conversion Psychology (Carried from v1)

These patterns are proven and carry forward:

1. **Scarcity:** "3 clients this quarter" — limited availability creates urgency
2. **Qualification barrier:** Apply modal asks screening questions — makes the prospect prove they deserve access
3. **Anti-sell:** "This isn't for everyone" — rejection increases perceived value
4. **Proof-first:** Lead with numbers, not promises
5. **Revenue over vanity:** Always tie metrics back to money

## Apply Modal Copy

### Question 1: Monthly Revenue
"What's your current monthly revenue?"
- Under $10K/month
- $10K - $50K/month
- $50K - $200K/month
- $200K+/month

If under $10K → Rejection: "We work best with established operators. When you're consistently at $10K+/month, reach out."

### Question 2: Content Goal
"What's your primary goal with content?"
- Drive direct revenue
- Build authority / audience
- Both

### On Qualification
"Application received. We'll review and reach out within 48 hours."

## Metric Presentation

- Revenue numbers ALWAYS lead
- Large, readable stat numbers (Clash Display Bold)
- Context line below the number explaining what it means
- Supporting text that tells the story, not just the data point

```
$83,212+
Revenue in 6 days

Trell was declining $8K/month. We deployed the system.
Six days later: $83K in gross revenue, 237 new users.
```
