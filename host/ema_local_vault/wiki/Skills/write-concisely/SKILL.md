# Goal

Write skill files, tool descriptions, and agent prompts that an agent can absorb without rereading.

## Inputs

- A draft (skill body, tool description, system prompt, comment)
- The audience (agent vs. human reader vs. both)
- The token budget for this artifact

## Workflow

1. **Cut every word that does not change the meaning.** "In order to" → "to". "Due to the fact that" → "because". "It should be noted that" → delete.
2. **Put statements in positive form.** "Do not use" → "avoid". "Not unimportant" → "important". Negative phrasing forces the reader to invert and lose attention.
3. **Use the active voice.** "The proposal is generated by the agent" → "the agent generates the proposal". Active voice has half the words and twice the clarity.
4. **One idea per sentence.** If the sentence has two `and`s or a semicolon, split it.
5. **Concrete over abstract.** "The system processes input" → "the parser reads JSON". Names are cheaper than categories.
6. **No filler.** "Great", "powerful", "robust", "elegant", "seamless" — strip them. They cost tokens and add zero information.
7. **Read it back at speed.** If your eyes slide off a sentence, the agent will too. Rewrite it.

## Output Contract

The final version is acceptable when:
- Every sentence advances the reader's model
- No sentence has both an `and` and a clause
- No filler adjectives remain
- The word count is at least 30% lower than the draft
- A non-expert can paraphrase the text after one read

## Common Failure Modes

- **Hedging.** "It might be worth considering whether perhaps..." is an apology, not a sentence.
- **Throat-clearing.** "This document covers..." Just cover it.
- **Sycophancy in docs.** "This excellent module..." Delete the adjective.
- **Repeating the heading in the first line.** The reader just read it.
- **Optimizing for length.** Short and unclear is worse than medium and clear. Cut words that hurt clarity, not words that help it.
