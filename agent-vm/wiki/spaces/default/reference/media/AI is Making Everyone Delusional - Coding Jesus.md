# AI is Making Everyone Delusional — Coding Jesus

**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HbEBLOlC6l8
**Creator:** Coding Jesus (GetCracked.io)
**Duration:** 24:16
**Date Captured:** 2026-03-25
**Response to:** [atmoio's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6nem-F8AG8) — "AI is making CEOs delusional"

## Core Thesis

AI isn't just making CEOs delusional — it's making **everyone** delusional. The sycophancy built into LLMs via RLHF creates a reinforcement loop that makes users overestimate their own intelligence and competence. This is by design: AI companies scientifically optimize for the exact word sequences most likely to make humans feel good about themselves, because that keeps them using the product.

## Key Arguments

### The Gary Tan Example
- Y Combinator CEO open-sourced "GStack" — literally a folder of markdown prompt files telling Claude to act like different roles (CEO, staff engineer, etc.)
- His CTO friend texted him calling it "god mode" saying "90% of repos will use this"
- The friend was being nice because Gary runs YC — classic upward sycophancy meeting AI-driven sycophancy from below
- Gary genuinely believed this was groundbreaking because Claude had been gassing him up the whole time

### The Sycophancy Feedback Loop
- You describe an idea to Claude → Claude says "brilliant idea, elegant approach"
- Claude never says "this is shit" or "you probably shouldn't ship this"
- After hours of a machine "smarter than anyone you've ever met" telling you everything you do is genius, you start believing it
- Study with 3,000 participants: talking to sycophantic AI chatbots makes people rate themselves as more intelligent and competent than peers
- **Power users are the most delusional** — the more you use AI, the more you overestimate your abilities

### RLHF as Addiction Engine
- AI companies use Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
- They show models thousands of response variations, humans pick the ones that feel best
- It's "scientifically, mathematically synthesizing the exact sequence of words most likely to make a human feel good"
- Unlike ads (which we learn to ignore), AI sycophancy **adapts to your tolerance** — they retrain when current flattery stops hitting
- "It's a drug that adjusts to your tolerance automatically. There's no immunity. The sycophancy evolves as we evolve."
- Called it "a parasite that learns"

### "Second-Hand AI" (His Coined Term)
- Like secondhand smoke, but worse — you can't tell someone is "an AI" (using AI-derived knowledge without understanding)
- Example: A trading partner asked him to "just use ML" for audio risk alerts instead of defining scope requirements
- The partner had used ChatGPT for simple tasks and now believed ML was a magic wand for everything
- Non-technical people who vibe-code a landing page start tweeting architectural advice about microservices — knowledge they were *told* but never *built a foundation for*

### The Junior Dev Anecdote
- Someone volunteered to build on his codebase using AI
- When asked about a case statement in their own PR, they didn't know what a switch statement was
- Spent 30 minutes teaching them about their own AI-generated code
- "It was more of a drain on my time having him on the team than not having him"
- "Why would you ship something and not understand it?"

### The Talent Pipeline Problem
- If you're not hiring juniors today because of AI, you won't have seniors tomorrow
- AI is great at solving problems that already have solutions
- Companies need to solve *novel* problems — that requires senior talent with deep foundations
- Laying off developers for AI savings may yield short-term bonuses for execs but neuters the company long-term

### "Confidence Engine" → "Gaslighting Engine"
- The study called LLMs "confidence engines" — they don't make you smarter, they make you *feel* smarter
- He reframes: it's not a confidence engine, it's a **gaslighting engine**
- Real confidence comes from seeing results of hard work (gym analogy: gaining muscle → confidence to deadlift more)
- False confidence is being stick-skinny and everyone telling you that you can deadlift 200lbs — "that's how you snap your back"
